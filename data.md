## Escalation Policies

This section details the escalation policies for various scenarios within Everest.

- **Project Job Extensions, Exceeding due date/timeline, or Hours Estimate**
  1.  Note: Always start this process after the official email, and start with level 1, and a level 1 person is compulsory in every escalation meeting
  2.  Level 1: Project Coordinator (PC)
      1.  Coordinate with JC and the Technical Coordinator, identify reasons for delays, and initiate corrective actions.
      2.  Extend the date with proper reason, informing Nidhi/Mitesh by email.
  3.  Level 2: Department Head
      1.  Review the extension request by the JC and PC.
      2.  Provide additional guidance or resource allocation if required.
  4.  Level 3: Partner Level
      1.  Analyse project impact and approve high-level decisions affecting due date/timeline or hours estimated.
  5.  Level 4: Kaushal Gajjar
      1.  Step in for critical decisions or when resolution is beyond the scope of the lower level

- **Delays in Client Payment Collection**
  1.  Note: Always start this process after official email and start with level 1 and level 1 person is compulsory in every escalation meeting
  2.  Level 1: Salesperson
      1.  Follow up with the client and document communication.
      2.  Attempt resolution via reminders through formal communication.
      3.  Inform Project Coordinator about the payment delays from the client.
  3.  Level 2: Project Coordinator
      1.  Review payment terms and assist with additional client communication along with the sales person.
      2.  Highlight risks associated with delayed payments. If needed, connect with Partner level/Kaushal to make decisions on further project execution.
  4.  Level 3: Partner Level
      1.  Align with organisational strategy for overdue accounts.
  5.  Level 4: Kaushal Gajjar
      1.  Finalise payment resolutions through high-level discussions with the client.

- **Persistent client feedback regarding poor-quality deliverables or unaddressed product defects**
  1.  Note: Always start this process after official email and start with level 1 and level 1 person is compulsory in every escalation meeting
  2.  Level 1: Project Coordinator
      1.  Investigate and coordinate with the technical coordinator & Engineering - Technical Coordinator to resolve quality issues promptly.
      2.  Assess gaps in the quality assurance process and implement corrective actions.
  3.  Level 2: Department Head
      1.  Review the concerns raised by the client and plan for corrective actions with the PC.
  4.  Level 3: Partner Level/Kaushal Gajjar
      1.  Address situations where the quality concerns pose a risk to the client relationship or company reputation.

- **Unplanned scope changes or excessive change requests (CRs/ARs) from the client impacting project timelines or budgets**
  1.  Note: Always start this process after official email and start with level 1 and level 1 person is compulsory in every escalation meeting.
  2.  Level 1: Project Coordinator
      1.  Discuss with the client to understand the impact and renegotiate timelines or costs.
      2.  Maintain a document with detailed project scope and CRs/ARs with timeline in coordination with JC.
      3.  Collaborate with clients to evaluate the CRs/ARs with a timeline.
  3.  Level 2: Department Head
      1.  Provide strategic guidance and approval for major scope adjustments.
      2.  If needed, collaborate with the client to finalise the scope, CRs/ARs and timeline.
  4.  Level 3: Partner Level / Kaushal Gajjar
      1.  Manage high-impact scope changes requiring organisational alignment.

- **Insufficient resources (team member, time, tools) to meet project requirements**
  1.  Note: Always start this process after official email and start with level 1 and level 1 person is compulsory in every escalation meeting.
  2.  Level 1 : Project Coordinator
      1.  Coordinate with the technical coordinator & engineering technical coordinator to analyse the resource requirements, such as personnel, time, or specific tools.
      2.  Reallocate existing team resources or adjust timelines to meet immediate demands.
      3.  Collaborate with management to secure additional resources across teams.
  3.  Level 2 : Department Head
      1.  Approve additional resources requests or make necessary adjustments.
  4.  Level 3 : Partner Level / Kaushal Gajjar
      1.  Prioritise critical projects and decide resource allocation at an organisational level.

- **Quality delivery becomes questionable and performing below standard**
  1.  Note: Always start this process after official email and start with level 1 and level 1 person is compulsory in every escalation meeting
  2.  Level 1 : Department Head
      1.  Please coordinate with the Department Head to analyse the performance of the resource and the quality of their work.
  3.  Level 2 : Partner Level / Kaushal Gajjar
      1.  Prioritise critical projects if they are handling any, and add senior support or replace them if needed.

- **No Ongoing Work or No Logged Hours by Employee**
  1.  Note: Always start this process after official email and start with level 1 and level 1 person is compulsory in every escalation meeting. This policy applies when an employee has not logged any hours on any assigned job.
  2.  Level 1 : Project Coordinator (PC)
      1.  Initiated when no hours have been logged for 15 consecutive days.
      2.  JC must notify the PC with job details, employee name, and duration of inactivity.
      3.  PC to assess the situation, verify any unassigned work, and take necessary corrective action.
  3.  Level 2 : Department Head & Sales person
      1.  Triggered if inactivity continues for 30 days.
      2.  PC must escalate the issue to their Department Head, highlighting prior communication and current status.
      3.  Department Head to evaluate resource utilisation, provide direction, or reassign work as needed.
  4.  Level 3 : Partner Level / Kaushal Gajjar & Sales person
      1.  Activated after 45 days of inactivity.
      2.  Department Head must escalate the matter with complete documentation to the Partner / Kaushal Gajjar.
      3.  Partner / Kaushal Gajjar to review broader implications, including staffing, billing, and team structure. Engaged for critical cases where inactivity remains unresolved or indicates systemic issues. Final decisions and structural interventions (if required) to be made at this level.

## Exit Policy

### Purpose

To ensure a smooth, controlled, and accountable transition of ownership when a team member exits the organization and holds one or more key roles in Everest—namely Project Coordinator (PC), Job Coordinator (JC), Sales Coordinator (SC), or Milestone Coordinator (MC).

This SOP ensures that all Everest-specific responsibilities, permissions, and roles are systematically reassigned, required knowledge transfer and pending reviews are completed, and system access is deactivated only after continuity of active operations is secured.

### High-Level Process Flow (Visual Overview)

(Image Here)

### Detailed Process Steps

1.  **Identify all active responsibilities**

    The Department Head (DH) must identify all active project, job:
    - Projects where the person is a PC
    - Jobs where they are a JC
    - Milestones where they are an MC
    - Sales records where they are an SC

    Once identified, the DH will raise a Support Ticket in Everest titled:
    - The ticket title must include: “Reassignment Request – \[Employee Name] Exit”- Project and Job names
    - Current role owner’s name
    - New assigned person’s name
    - Any special notes (pending hours, client handover items, etc.)

    The Everest Admin Team will process the reassignments as per this SOP.

2.  **Pre-reassignment actions**

    Before any reassignment begins:
    - Confirm that all pending timesheets, milestones, and approvals are reviewed or resolved.

    - Ensure all project documentation, client communications, and internal notes are passed to the successor.

    - The exiting member must complete a Knowledge Transfer (KT) with their successor (details below).

3.  **Post-update validation**

    After reassignment:
    - Verify that no active projects, jobs, or milestones remain under the exiting member.

    - Confirm that new owners have required permissions in the Everest.

4.  **Account deactivation request**

    After all reassignments, validations, and KTs are completed

    The Department Head (DH) must raise a new Support Ticket in Everest titled: “Account Deactivation – \[Employee Name] Exit”
    - This ticket confirms that, all role handovers are completed
    - Pending hours and reviews are cleared
    - The account is ready to be made inactive

    The Everest Admin Team will mark the employee as Inactive (data retained for reporting) and close the ticket

## Glossary

| Term                     | Short Description                                                                                                              |
| :----------------------- | :----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Everest                  | Internal operations platform used to manage projects, jobs, resources, timesheets, billing, and performance.                   |
| Client                   | External organization or individual for whom work is delivered and billed.                                                     |
| Organization             | Internal or legal entity under which employees, projects, and jobs are managed.                                                |
| Entity                   | A business unit or operational unit used for reporting, billing, or compliance.                                                |
| Project                  | A high-level container representing a client engagement or internal initiative; contains one or more jobs.                     |
| Job                      | The smallest executable unit of work where timelines, budgets, timesheets, and billing are tracked.                            |
| Job Group (Contract)     | Logical grouping of jobs under a single contractual agreement with the client.                                                 |
| Fixed Job                | Job with a predefined scope and budget, billed as a fixed amount regardless of hours spent.                                    |
| Hourly Job               | Job billed based on actual approved hours worked; no fixed budget required.                                                    |
| Dedicated Job            | Job where specific resources are assigned for a fixed recurring period, usually monthly or long-term.                          |
| Bucket Job               | Prepaid job with fixed hours that can be consumed until hours expire or the end date is reached.                               |
| Recurring Job            | Job with automatic recurring billing at a defined frequency (weekly, monthly, yearly) until closed.                            |
| Moving slow              | Job flag automatically applied when no timesheet hours are logged for 15&nbsp; days,&nbsp;indicating slow or stalled progress. |
| On hold                  | Job flag automatically applied when no timesheet hours are logged for 45 days,&nbsp;indicating the job is paused or inactive.  |
| Billable                 | A billing unit that defines how work is invoiced and enables time logging for a specific period.                               |
| Billable Date            | The date on which a billable is considered for invoicing or billing action.                                                    |
| Due days                 | Number of days after the billing date within which payment is expected from the client.                                        |
| Due Date                 | System-calculated date when payment is due, based on Billing Date + Due Days.                                                  |
| Start Date               | The date from which the job or billable becomes active and eligible for timesheet logging.                                     |
| End Date                 | The last valid date until which work can be logged against a job or billable.                                                  |
| Forecast Date            | Expected future date when a billing event is planned to complete.                                                              |
| Collected Date           | The date on which payment is actually received from the client.                                                                |
| Timesheet                | Daily record of hours logged by employees against jobs or milestones.                                                          |
| Available Hours          | Total workable hours available for an employee within a given period.                                                          |
| Logged Hours             | Hours entered by employees in the timesheet but not yet reviewed or approved.                                                  |
| Approved Hours           | Timesheet hours that have been reviewed and approved by the responsible authority.                                             |
| Spent Hours              | Total hours already used or consumed against a job or budget.                                                                  |
| Consumed Hours           | Hours deducted from a&nbsp; job based on approved time logs.                                                                   |
| Utilization              | Measure of how effectively available or billable hours are used.                                                               |
| Project Coordinator (PC) | Owner of the project responsible for planning, coordination, and overall delivery governance.                                  |
| Sales Coordinator (SC)   | Role responsible for client onboarding, commercials, and coordination with delivery teams.                                     |
| Budget                   | Planned allocation of cost or hours used to track utilization and overrun/underrun.                                            |
| Utilization              | Measure of how effectively available or billable hours are used.                                                               |
| In Review                | Job status indicating work is completed and hours are pending review or closure actions.                                       |
| Backlog                  | Job state where setup is complete but execution has not yet started.                                                           |
| Extension                | Approved continuation of a job beyond its original end date.                                                                   |
| Invoice                  | Financial document generated from approved billables and sent to the client.                                                   |
| Internal Project         | Non-client project used for internal initiatives such as R&D, training, or operations.                                         |

## Project Lifecycle from Odoo to Everest

The project lifecycle in Everest begins with a lead assignment in Odoo and ends with project closure in Everest after all jobs and billables are completed. Below is the step-by-step lifecycle, including roles and responsibilities.

### 1. Lead Stage (Odoo)

**Process**:

- A Sales Person is assigned a lead in Odoo.
- Lead contains initial client requirement details.

**Roles & Responsibilities:**

- **Sales Person:**
  - Contact the client.
  - Qualify the lead and collect requirements.
  - Convert lead into an Opportunity in Odoo.

### 2. Opportunity Stage (Odoo)

**Process:**

- After qualification, the lead becomes an Opportunity.
- Opportunity includes client needs, estimated budget, and potential project scope.

**Roles & Responsibilities:**

- **Sales Person:**
  - Record potential deal value and scope in Odoo.
  - Follow up and confirm client interest.

### 3. Project Creation (Everest)

**Process:**

- Once the opportunity is confirmed, a Project is created in Everest.
- The Project represents a major client engagement.

**Roles & Responsibilities:**

- **Sales Coordinator (SC):**
  - Create a new Project in Everest with Client, SC, PC, NDA Level, and Industry.
  - Define the billables and inform to Finance person to create the billables.
  - Discuss the jobs with PC and then PC will create the jobs.

- **Project Coordinator (PC):**
  - Review the project and jobs.

### 4. Project Estimation

**Process:**

- Initial estimation is done to define the project cost, timeline, and resources.
- Allocation of departments and grades for budgeting.

**Roles & Responsibilities:**

- **Finance Team:**
  - Add allocation hours in the Budget (department-wise and grade-wise).
- **PC:**
  - Review and validate estimation.

### 5. How to prepare an Estimation

At 7Span, project estimation is a structured process that helps define expected efforts in hours across different departments and roles. It serves as a foundation for budgeting, planning sprints, and aligning client expectations.

We follow a department-wise estimation strategy where estimations are broken down into units like development, UI/UX, QA, DevOps, and project management. Additionally, each estimation line item is tagged with a default grade level unless otherwise specified by the estimator.

**Core Principles**

1.  Department-wise Estimation

    Each module or task in a project is categorized under a department. The common departments include:
    - 🧑‍💻 Development (Frontend, Backend)

    - 🎨 Design (UI/UX)

    - 🔍 Quality Assurance (QA)

    - 🧪 DevOps & Deployment

    - 📅 Project Management (PM)

    Each department is assigned estimated hours independently, ensuring clarity of scope, responsibility, and resourcing.

2.  Default Grade Logic

    To standardize estimation and align with resource planning, we assign a default grade level to each estimation based on the department.

| Department          | Default Grade |
| :------------------ | :------------ |
| Development         | A3            |
| Design              | A3            |
| Quality Assurance   | A3            |
| DevOps & Deployment | A3            |
| Project Management  | M1A           |

**If the estimator explicitly provides a grade, it overrides the default.**

When assigning any dedicated resource to a job in Everest, it is mandatory to specify the exact grade of the person who will be working on that job. This ensures accurate budgeting, billing, and performance tracking based on the individual's role and experience level.

- Once the estimation is finalised, discuss it with SC and then SC will share it with the client.
- Once client approves the estimation, it serves as the foundation for generating a Sales Order in Odoo after confirming it with the estimator.
- This Sales Order reflects the departmental breakdown, estimated hours, and budget allocation derived from the original estimation document.
- After the Sales Order is created, the PC takes responsibility for creating the corresponding Job inside Everest.
- The job is initialised with the same scope and structure as defined in the Sales Order, and the budget is assigned department-wise accordingly by the Finance team once the Job is created by the PC.
- This ensures full alignment between estimation, sales, finance, and project execution.

## Logs

### Overview

The Timesheet Logs module in Everest enables employees to record the actual working hours spent on specific jobs or milestones. It ensures accurate effort tracking, supports billing, and provides reliable data for project progress, utilization, and revenue reporting.

### Key Purposes of Timesheet Logging:

- Maintain accurate records of employee work hours.
- Track time spent per job, milestone, and project.
- Enable billing and payroll calculations.
- Support project performance analysis and utilization tracking.

### 1. Timesheet Logging Rules

Logging Window:

- Employees can log hours for the current day and up to the previous seven days.

Daily Logging:

- Hours should be logged at the end of each workday to maintain accuracy.

Late Logs:

- If hours are missed within the seven-day window, the employee can unlock the specific past day and log hours without approval.

Non-Editable Beyond Window:

- Timesheet entries older than seven days cannot be added or modified unless they are unlocked, as per the late log rule.

### 2. Mandatory Conditions for Logging Hours (Updated)

From now on, timesheet logging is allowed only when BOTH conditions below are met:

1.  The log date falls within the Job’s Start and End Date
2.  A Billable exists and its date range covers the same log date

⚠️ If a billable is missing or does not cover the selected date, hour logging will be blocked, even if the job dates are valid.

### 3. Add Logs – Process

Responsible Person: Employee

Path: Work → Timesheet → Logs&nbsp; -> “+ Add Logs”

Steps:

1.  Select the Date of work.
2.  Choose the Project and relevant Job or Milestone.
3.  Enter the Hours Worked.
4.  Add a Brief Description of the work performed.
5.  Click Save.

### 4. What to Do If You Cannot Log Hours

If you’re unable to log hours for a valid job period:

1.  Contact your Job Coordinator (JC)
2.  Inform them that:
3.  The JC will coordinate with the PC and Finance Team
4.  Once the billable date range is corrected, logging will be enabled

### 5. Finance Responsibility – Billables (Important Update)

The Finance Team is responsible for:

## Tasks

### Overview

The Tasks feature in Everest ensures that all mandatory actions in the project and job lifecycle are completed on time. Tasks are automatically assigned to relevant roles whenever an action is required, such as creating a job, configuring a job, creating billables, deciding whether to extend or close a job, resolving overdue billables, or completing missing timesheet logs.

Each task remains open until the required action is completed and must be closed manually. A task cannot be closed unless its associated action has been successfully completed. This ensures accountability, prevents incomplete setups, and maintains a smooth and controlled workflow across projects and jobs.

### Key Purpose of Tasks

- Ensure mandatory workflow steps are completed on time
- Improve accountability across different roles
- Prevent incomplete setups ( jobs, job configs, billables, or timesheet)
- Ensure billables are processed on time
- Ensure employees maintain regular timesheet logging
- Provide clear action reminders during job and project lifecycle

### How Tasks Work (Lifecycle-Based)

##### 1. Task on Project Creation (Finance Team)

When the Finance Team creates a project:

A task is automatically assigned:

Task: Create Job

This task cannot be closed until at least one job is created under the project.

Rule: Task remains open until the required job is created.

##### 2. Task After Job Creation (Finance Team)

Once the job is created:

A new task is assigned:

Task: Configure Job & Create Billable

This includes:

- Adding Job Configuration
- Creating required Billable(s)

Rule: Task cannot be closed until:

- Job configuration is completed, and
- At least one valid billable is created.

##### 3. Task When Job Moves to In Review (JC)

When a job moves to In Review:

A task is assigned to the Job Coordinator (JC):

Task: Extend or Close Job

JC must:

- Extend the job, or
- Close the job.

Rule: Task remains open until one action is completed.

##### 4. Task for Billable Overdue (Sales Coordinator)

When a billable becomes overdue, a task is automatically assigned to the Sales Coordinator.

Task:&nbsp; Billable&nbsp;Overdue&nbsp;

Sales Coordinator must:

- Move billable status to Billed, or
- Move billable status to Collected, or
- Extend the billable.

Rule: Task cannot be closed until billable status is corrected or extended.

##### 5. Task for Missing Timesheet Logs (Employees)

If an employee has not logged timesheet hours for previous days, tasks are assigned automatically.

Example:
If today is 29-01-2026, and logs are missing for 22-01-2026 or earlier, tasks will be generated.

Each missing date creates a separate task.

Example:
If logs are missing for 3 days → 3 tasks are created.

Employee must:

- Log timesheet for that date, or
- Acknowledge the task.

Rule: Task can only be closed after logging or acknowledgement.

### Task Closure Rules

- Tasks must always be closed manually.
- Task closure is allowed only after completing the required action.
- Tasks cannot be closed if:
- Job is not created
- Job configuration is incomplete
- Billables are missing
- Job action is pending in In Review
- Billable remains overdue
- Timesheet logs are missing or not acknowledged

Task closure acts as confirmation that the workflow step is complete.

### Who Gets Tasks?

| Role                 | Task Type                        |
| :------------------- | :------------------------------- |
| Finance Team         | Create Job                       |
| Finance Team         | Configure Job & Create Billables |
| Job Coordinator (JC) | Extend or Close Job              |
| Sales Coordinator    | Resolve Overdue Billables        |
| Employee             | Missing Timesheet Logs           |

### Things to Consider

- Tasks are action-driven, not reminders.
- Tasks cannot be closed without completing the required action.
- Tasks improve billing discipline and operational accuracy.
- Timesheet tasks are generated date-wise.
- Always review task details before closing.

## Handover from sales to delivery team

### 1. Purpose

This SOP defines the standardized process for handing over a Won deal from Sales and Pre-Sales to Delivery, ensuring that:

- Delivery scope and commercial scope are fully aligned
- A delivery budget and allocation are formally recorded by the Finance function
- Delivery execution begins only after budget confirmation
- Clear accountability is established across Sales, Pre-Sales, Finance, and Delivery
- Any scope or budget change follows a controlled approval process

This process acts as a binding operational contract between teams once the budget is recorded.

### 2. Scope

#### In Scope

- Deals marked as Won
- Creation of projects, jobs, billables, allocations, timelines, and grades
- Budget confirmation and lock by Finance
- Formal handover to Delivery

#### Out of Scope

- Pre-sales estimation activities
- Contract negotiation and legal approvals
- Post-delivery change management

### 3. Roles & Responsibilities

| Role              | Responsibility                                                |
| :---------------- | :------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Sales             | Owns commercial terms, pricing, discounts, and billing model  |
| Pre-Sales         | Owns solution scope, assumptions, and delivery approach       |
| Finance Executive | Records and locks budget, creates jobs and billables          |
| Delivery Lead     | Accepts handover and executes delivery within approved budget |

Note: Roles are authoritative; individuals and tools may change without impacting this SOP.

### 4. Required Handover Artifacts (Mandatory Inputs)

The following information must be documented and shared before Delivery execution can begin.
These artifacts act as the minimum binding input for Delivery planning and Finance budget recording.

#### 4.1 Minimum Data for Delivery to Start

Sales and Pre-Sales are responsible for ensuring the following details are captured in the standard [handover template](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1kXxDWoS7dHQVxlUC3kl3Fma3Ke9jG1LtuT2FnBudWu4/edit?tab=t.0):

- Delivery scope and deliverables
- Assumptions and exclusions
- Target timelines and milestones
- Required departments and skill levels
- Engagement type (fixed / hourly / dedicated / recurring / bucket)
- Any known delivery constraints or dependencies

Control Rule:
Delivery may reject the handover if this information is incomplete or unclear.

#### 4.2 Delivery → Finance: Job Creation Input

Delivery Lead is responsible for sharing the following details with the Finance function in the standard [template](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1kXxDWoS7dHQVxlUC3kl3Fma3Ke9jG1LtuT2FnBudWu4/edit?tab=t.6wvnhfpkkspm):

- Project and job structure
- Department-wise allocation requirements
- Grades / roles required
- Start and end dates
- Delivery milestones (if applicable)

Important Principle:
Delivery provides structure only, not commercial or financial values.

## Selection of job type

### When to create Hourly Job

Use Hourly when the scope of work is not clearly defined, and client agrees to pay based on actual time logged.

#### 💡 How it works

You log hours → client pays for that time → amount is variable.

#### ✅ Scenarios

- Small tasks & bug fixes: Client reports minor issues or enhancements and agrees to hourly billing.
- Exploratory or R&D work: Time boxed discovery, prototyping, or feasibility checks with no clear scope.
- Ongoing backend/frontend support: Design/devs log hours as needed across different parts of the project.
- Design iterations or UI polishing: When UI/UX team is refining based on subjective feedback or user testing.
- Consulting or DevOps Setup: Includes cloud setup, infra improvements, or configuration work on-call.
- Extra internal efforts needed: Internal sprint where effort is unclear but needs to be tracked.

#### ⚠️ Reminder

Even if you work 50 hours or 5 hours, you’ll be paid for exactly that.

### When to create Dedicated Job

Use Dedicated when the scope is open-ended, but the client pays for a fixed amount of team time (usually per month or week), regardless of actual usage.

#### 💡 How it works

You book a resource → client pays fixed price for reserved time → output can vary.

#### ✅ Scenarios

- Resource Augmentation: Client wants a specific developer/designer full-time or part-time per month.
- Retainers or monthly support: Ongoing involvement in sprints, QA, or release support from fixed team.
- Embedded team in client processes: When your team joins client stand ups and works like internal resources.
- Design or Marketing retainers: Client keeps 7Span on contract for design/social/email tasks every month.
- Time-share across 2-3 projects: One senior dev is working on multiple client apps with fixed monthly capacity.

#### ⚠️ Reminder

Client pays even if there's less work – you’re being reserved, not just used.

### When to create Fixed Cost Job

Use Fixed when the scope of work and total amount is clearly defined in advance. You get paid the agreed price, even if you spend more time.

#### 💡 How it works

You commit to delivery → client pays agreed amount → risk of overwork is yours.

#### ✅ Scenarios

- Landing page or brochure site: Clear UI designs provided, ~5-10 pages to deliver in 2-3 weeks.
- MVP or App Modules: Client wants a defined output like “user login + dashboard + admin panel”."
- Product milestone releases: You break a big product into multiple fixed-scope phases and treat each as Fixed.
- Internal tools or utility apps: Well-understood features like ticketing system, resume parser, etc.
- Webflow/WordPress/Shopify builds: Site creation with predefined assets, plugins, and payment integrations.

#### ⚠️ Reminder

If you spend 60 hours instead of estimated 40 – you don’t get more money.

### When to Create a Recurring Job

Use a Recurring Job when the work follows a fixed schedule and repeats at regular intervals, with billing happening automatically based on the selected cycle (weekly, monthly, yearly)

#### 💡 How it works

You define a billing cycle → billing repeats automatically → job continues until closed.

#### ✅ Scenarios

- Managed services: Ongoing website, server, or application maintenance billed monthly
- Domain or hosting renewals: Annual or monthly renewals with predictable billing
- Subscription-based services: SEO, content posting, monitoring, or analytics reporting
- Long-term support contracts: Client pays a fixed recurring amount for continued availability
- Compliance or monitoring services: Regular checks, backups, or security audits

⚠️ Reminder
Billing continues as per the schedule until the job is closed. Once closed, recurring billing stops immediately.&nbsp;&nbsp;

### When to Create a Bucket Job

Use a Bucket Job when the client wants to prepay for a fixed number of hours and consume them flexibly over time.

&nbsp; 💡 How it works

Client prepays hours → team consumes hours as needed → job ends when hours or end date is reached.

✅ Scenarios

- Small tasks & bug fixes: Minor changes requested occasionally
- Support & maintenance work: On-demand help without fixed schedules
- Marketing & branding tasks: Design changes, campaign tweaks, content updates
- Ad-hoc development requests: Short, unplanned work items
- Internal or external support pools: Shared hours across small activities

⚠️ Reminder

- Once hours are consumed or the end date is reached, the job moves to In Review
- Additional work requires creating a new Bucket Job
